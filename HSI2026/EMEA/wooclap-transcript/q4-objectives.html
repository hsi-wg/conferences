Q4 - Network objectives

Question

What research-coordination and capacity-building objectives should the network formulate for the proposal?

Wooclap results

Research coordination responses were led by a joint HSI research agenda (4), educational material for universities (3), exchange of domain good practices for AI systems development (3), a country-level HSI landscape survey (2), and roadblocks in sociotechnical-system study/design/assessment (2). Capacity-building responses included a global HSI exam/certification (3), HSI heuristics (3), cross-domain mapping of EU and wider research projects (2), cross-domain knowledge transfer (2), standards and trust-in-AI work (1 each), practices for transdisciplinary work (1), plus use cases and validation guidance.

Discussion synthesis

  • Dimitri framed the objective split as research coordination versus capacity building, while asking whether the network should focus on common understanding, methodology, assessment, or dissemination.
  • A joint research agenda was understood as a reason for networking: identifying who is doing what, defining key changes and steps, and creating a common outcome rather than creating a single method.
  • Ricardo clarified that a COST action should understand, synthesize, provoke, and open paths for research projects; it is not itself a research project.
  • Training schools were treated as both activities and tangible outputs because they require prepared materials and can build capability face to face.
  • A recurring objective was to move HSI earlier into engineering practice and culture, rather than treating human factors as a late addition.

Additional information

Ricardo described the network as producing a foundation that remains relevant over time. The proposal should therefore express coordination outputs and capability-building mechanisms, not just a list of topics.
