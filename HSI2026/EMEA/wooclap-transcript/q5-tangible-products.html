Q5 - Tangible products

Question

Which tangible products should the network prioritize, given limited resources?

Wooclap results

The ranking was: HSI Engineering Workflows Playbook (156), Guidelines for HSI Influence on AI Development (148), Reference Book/Database of HSI Use Cases (132), Field Guide for Transdisciplinary HSI Practices (111), Reference HSI Viewpoints (108), HSI Heuristics Reference Book for Safety/Mission-Critical Architecting (101), Training Materials/Courses (101), Observatory on Governance/Regulatory Frameworks (87), and HSI R&D Agenda policy white papers (56).

Discussion synthesis

  • Ricardo explicitly argued for two or three products rather than many, because resources are limited and tangible outputs force scope, focus, action, and integration.
  • The result shows a strong preference for practical artifacts that people can use in day-to-day work, especially workflows, guidance, use cases, and transdisciplinary practice.
  • Ricardo was surprised that the policy/R&D agenda scored lower than practical products. He interpreted this as a pragmatic orientation rather than a rejection of strategic work.
  • Training materials were clarified as potentially including a book, but more importantly as reusable material for funded summer or spring schools.
  • A proposal for HSI training should combine artifacts with an actual delivery mechanism, especially face-to-face schools.

Additional information

The products are not independent of the activities: training schools produce and use training materials; workshops and meetings generate use cases and practice exchange; an agenda can coordinate future projects even if it is not the most popular standalone artifact.

Caveat

The displayed ranking is a points allocation, not a commitment that every top-ranked product will be delivered. Selection still requires feasibility and alignment with COST funding rules.
